# On the Proximity of Cosmic Mass–Radius Ratios to Black Hole Limits

![Research](https://img.shields.io/badge/Research-Astrophysics-blue)

---

## 🔭 Overview

This repository contains the materials for an independent research project investigating **how the sizes and masses of cosmic objects relate to the extreme limits that could cause gravitational collapse into black holes**.  

Black holes are defined by the **Schwarzschild radius**, a critical threshold where gravity becomes so strong that nothing—not even light—can escape. This work explores how ordinary stars, planets, and compact objects compare to this limit and highlights the interplay between **classical Newtonian reasoning** and **relativistic physics**.

---

## 🧪 Research Focus

- **Schwarzschild Proximity**: Examining mass–radius ratios of stars, neutron stars, and planets relative to black hole formation thresholds.  
- **Newtonian vs. Relativistic Analysis**: Contrasting simplified escape-velocity calculations with relativistic corrections to clarify when naive interpretations are misleading.  
- **Physical Interpretability**: Investigating where mathematical proximity to the Schwarzschild radius does **not imply actual physical collapse** due to internal pressure or relativistic effects.  
- **Visualization**: Schematic diagrams and illustrative plots mapping cosmic objects in the mass–radius parameter space.  

---

## 📊 Key Figures

- **Mass–Radius Diagram**: Displays the parameter space of stellar objects relative to Schwarzschild limits. Ordinary stars lie safely below the collapse threshold, while neutron stars approach it.  
- **Photon Trajectory Near Black Hole**: Hypothetical illustration of photon motion near the event horizon, emphasizing the difference between naive classical expectations and general relativistic behavior.
